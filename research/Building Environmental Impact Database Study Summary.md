# Building Environmental Impact Database: Plain Language Summary

## What This Paper Is About

This paper describes a **large database of environmental impact data for buildings** in the US and Canada. Think of it as a comprehensive spreadsheet that tracks how much carbon and other pollutants go into constructing buildings.

## The Core Problem They're Solving

Buildings create a lot of environmental damage—not just from running them (heating, cooling, electricity), but from **building them in the first place** (mining materials, manufacturing, transporting, construction). Currently:

- There's no standardized way different companies measure this impact
- Results from one company's analysis aren't easily comparable to another's
- Most publicly available data is either incomplete or too vague to be useful
- There's basically no good North American database that combines material use data with environmental impact data

## What They Built

The researchers collected data from **30 architecture and engineering firms** across North America, gathering information on **292 real building projects** representing nearly 5 million square meters of floor space. For each building, they recorded:

- What materials were used and how much
- The environmental impacts (carbon emissions, water pollution, air pollution, etc.)
- Building details (size, structure type, location, etc.)

## Key Findings

- Average embodied carbon (from construction) ranges from about 84 to 2,160 kg of CO₂ per square meter
- Concrete and steel are the biggest contributors to environmental impact
- The structural frame of buildings accounts for the largest share of impact
- Most impact comes from manufacturing materials, not transporting or disposing of them

## Why It Matters

This dataset lets architects, builders, and policymakers:

- Compare the environmental impact of different design choices
- Set targets to reduce impact
- Understand which materials and building types are most damaging
- Make more sustainable decisions before construction begins

It's essentially the first comprehensive, publicly available, apples-to-apples comparison of building environmental impacts in North America.
